
# Stakeholder Discovery and Requirements Plan

## Step 1: Stakeholder Identification

### Stakeholder Group 1: Property Professionals
Property professionals use the platform to manage and
promote property listings. This group may include real
estate agents, property managers, and property owners.

### Stakeholder Group 2: Local Sponsors
Local sponsors are businesses that may want to advertise
their products or services to people browsing property
listings.

## Known Facts
- The platform has two stakeholder groups: property
  professionals and local sponsors.
- The project requires identifying stakeholder needs.
- The project requires a content approval workflow.
- The project includes planning a minimum viable product.

## Assumptions
- Property professionals want an easy way to create
  and manage property listings.
- Local sponsors want to promote their businesses.
- Both groups want a simple, user-friendly platform.
- Property professionals need to update listing details.
- Sponsors want advertisements approved before publication.
- Both groups benefit from mobile accessibility.


## Step 2: Stakeholder Discovery Questions

The following questions will help identify the needs
of property professionals and local sponsors.

1. How do you currently manage property listings
   or business advertisements?

2. What challenges do you face when managing
   listings or advertisements?

3. Who are the primary buyers, renters, or local
   customers you are trying to reach through
   these listings?

4. What information do you believe should be
   included in a listing or advertisement?

5. What process, if any, do you need before content
   goes live (such as draft reviews, approval
   steps, or instant publishing)?

6. How do you know if a listing or advertisement
   is performing well for your business?

7. Are there any specific tools, layout options,
   or assistive features (such as high contrast,
   large text, or keyboard navigation) that make
   platforms easier for you to use?

8. What features would you consider most important
   for the platform's first version?

   
### AI Review: Google Gemini

Google Gemini was used to evaluate the eight
stakeholder discovery questions for bias, leading
language, clarity, and privacy concerns.

Gemini found that questions 1, 2, 4, and 8 were
neutral and did not require changes.

The following improvements were made:

- Question 3: Clarified the intended audience for
  property professionals and local sponsors.
- Question 5: Removed the assumption that all
  stakeholders need a formal approval process.
- Question 6: Allowed stakeholders to describe
  informal as well as formal success measures.
- Question 7: Added examples to make accessibility
  easier to understand.

No major privacy-invasive questions were identified.

The revised questions will be used as the final
stakeholder interview guide.

## Step 7: AI Studio Theme Analysis

Google AI Studio was used to analyze the
de-identified notes from two simulated
stakeholder interviews.

### Theme 1: Marketing Practices and Challenges

Property professionals use spreadsheets and
listing websites, while local sponsors use
social media. Both experience challenges
with their existing promotional workflows.

### Theme 2: Target Audience Outreach

Property professionals want to reach buyers
and renters. Local sponsors want to reach
customers living in or moving to the area.

### Theme 3: Content and Approval

Property professionals need property details,
photos, and listing management tools.

Local sponsors need business information,
advertisement creation, and preview options.

Both groups want to review content before
publication.

### Theme 4: Usability and Accessibility

Property professionals prefer mobile-friendly
layouts and readable text.

Local sponsors prefer simple navigation
and mobile accessibility.

### Theme 5: Success Measurement

Property professionals evaluate success
through inquiries and property tours.

Local sponsors evaluate success through
customer inquiries and engagement.

### Verification Against Original Notes

Each of the five themes was compared with
the original simulated interview notes.

1. Marketing challenges:
   Supported by both stakeholders' descriptions
   of their current tools and difficulties.

2. Target audience:
   Supported by the descriptions of buyers,
   renters, and nearby customers.

3. Content and approval:
   Supported by both stakeholders' content
   requirements and review preferences.

4. Usability and accessibility:
   Supported by requests for mobile-friendly
   interfaces, readable text, and navigation.

5. Success measurement:
   Supported by references to inquiries,
   property tours, and engagement.

### AI Accuracy Review

All five themes were supported by the
simulated interview notes.

Two interpretations required clarification:

- The notes support mobile usability and
  readability, but do not prove that all
  accessibility needs have been identified.
- The notes identify ways to measure success,
  but do not explicitly require automated
  performance analytics.

These interpretations were clarified to
avoid adding unsupported requirements.

The findings remain working hypotheses
because the interviews were simulated
rather than conducted with real participants.

## Step 10: Acceptance Criteria

The following acceptance criteria use the
Given/When/Then format for high-priority
user stories.

### US-01: Create Property Listings
Priority: High

Given a property professional is signed in,
When they enter the required property details
and submit a new listing,
Then the system saves the listing with a
pending review status.

### US-02: Edit Property Listings
Priority: High

Given a property professional has an existing
listing that they are authorized to edit,
When they update the property information
and save the changes,
Then the system saves the updated information
and marks the changes as pending review.

### US-04: Review Listings
Priority: High

Given a property professional has completed
a draft property listing,
When they open the listing preview,
Then the system displays the listing details
for review before submission.

### US-05: Create Advertisements
Priority: High

Given a local sponsor is signed in,
When they enter the required advertisement
information and submit it,
Then the system saves the advertisement with
a pending review status.

and testing of the platform.

## Step 13: Content Approval Workflow

The platform will use a content approval
workflow to help ensure that property listings
and sponsor advertisements are accurate and
appropriate before publication.

### Property Listing Approval Process

1. A property professional creates or edits
   a property listing.

2. The system saves the listing as a draft.

3. The property professional reviews the
   listing for accuracy.

4. The listing is submitted for approval
   and receives a Pending Review status.

5. An authorized reviewer checks the property
   details, descriptions, prices, photos,
   and permissions to use the content.

6. If approved, the listing is published.

7. If changes are required, the listing is
   returned to the property professional
   for corrections and resubmission.

### Local Sponsor Advertisement Approval Process

1. A local sponsor creates an advertisement
   containing business information.

2. The system saves the advertisement as
   a draft.

3. The sponsor previews the advertisement
   before submission.

4. The advertisement is submitted for
   approval and receives Pending Review status.

5. An authorized reviewer checks the business
   information, promotional claims, and
   content permissions.

6. If approved, the advertisement is published.

7. If corrections are needed, the advertisement
   is returned to the sponsor for revisions
   and resubmission.

### Content Approval Rules

- Only authorized reviewers can approve content.
- Content must be accurate and appropriate.
- Promotional claims should be supported by
  evidence and must not be misleading.
- Property photos and advertisements must have
  appropriate usage permissions.
- Rejected content must include a reason.
- Revised content must be reviewed again
  before publication.
- Only approved versions may be published.
- Approval decisions and changes should be logged.

### Approval Statuses

- Draft: Content is being created or edited.
- Pending Review: Content is awaiting approval.
- Approved: Content has passed review.
- Changes Requested: Content requires corrections.
- Published: Approved content is publicly visible.

### Workflow Validation

This approval workflow is a proposed design
based on the assignment requirements and
simulated stakeholder feedback.

It will require further testing and validation
before implementation.
